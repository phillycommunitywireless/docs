# Guiding Principles

PCW’s goal is to perform successful, good work in the safest and most efficient way possible, while facilitating learning for our community. Here are some guiding principles to help achieve this goal:

1. Occam’s razor: keep things simple. Don’t do anything unsafe that isn’t necessary. Try to do the easiest version of each task.
1. In line with Occam’s razor, reduce variables that introduce new or challenging factors where possible. Each install already involves a wide range of variables: the physical space, the people involved, the equipment and tools being used, the environment, and more. In consideration of how much needs to be managed during an install, do not introduce multiple new or experimental things at once. Make changes iteratively.
1. Each install should have an identified minimum viable product (MVP), identified during the prep phase. Sometimes this means just getting a PhillyWisper LiteBeam online. Sometimes this means installing just one access point (AP).
1. Focus on the install. An install is work. While we want it to be enjoyable, it isn’t an environment for “hanging out” or being on our phones unrelated to the install. Don’t get stuck in people or events external to the work being done.
1. Pause to think. At any decision point, stop and ask (yourself and/or your fellow installers), “What is the purpose of doing this? Is there a better way to do this that I’m not immediately seeing?”
1. Prioritize teamwork and staying on the same page. Make eye contact when speaking to one another. Use the “looping” technique to ensure understanding — listen to someone, and then restate what you heard back to ensure that you both understand. Do not change plans without communicating those changes to the team. If splitting up, use phones to communicate.
1. Check-in repeatedly with how people are doing. Scan environments and use situational awareness to identify potential pain points to check in about: the weather, the working conditions, heights, insects, etc. Have snacks and water accessible.
1. Involve each other. Even a first-time volunteer can participate in a meaningful way by learning to crimp, plugging in a device, etc. Invite everyone to have a role in the work.

## Install Process

PCW installations at new sites are broken up into the following phases:

1. Virtual Line-of-Sight Check
1. [Building Assessment](../buildingassessment.md)
1. Plan and Preparation
1. Installation
1. Post-installation
